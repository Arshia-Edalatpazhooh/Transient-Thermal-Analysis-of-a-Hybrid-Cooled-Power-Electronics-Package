This project investigates the transient thermal management of high-power electronics packages subjected to intense periodic thermal pulsing by modeling two-dimensional heat conduction across an alloy steel substrate equipped with a hybrid cooling system. The governing unsteady two-dimensional heat diffusion equation is discretized using both explicit and fully implicit finite volume formulations across an isotropic spatial grid incorporating internal heat-generating cavity walls, liquid cooling channels, external forced air convection, and adiabatic boundaries. Computational solvers were developed in MATLAB and Python utilizing sparse coefficient matrix assembly and precomputed sparse LU factorization for ultra-fast time-stepping, alongside comprehensive spatial and temporal mesh independence analyses cross-verified against commercial CFD simulations in ANSYS Fluent. Numerical verification established an analytical stability threshold of delta_t_max = delta_x^2 / [3.7975e-6 * (4 + 6*delta_x)] for the explicit scheme, while mesh refinement from delta_x = 0.015 m to delta_x = 0.001 m confirmed asymptotic spatial convergence to 40.12 °C. Under a peak oscillating heat flux of 25,000 W/m² with a 300-second cycle period, the critical thermal bottleneck midpoint between the cavities achieved a maximum operating temperature of 40.58 °C during the initial 30 minutes, successfully maintaining a 34.42 °C safety margin below the 75.0 °C thermal breakdown limit. Extended multi-cycle time integration confirmed that the system achieves a stable quasi-steady periodic state once cycle-to-cycle temperature variations fall below 0.05 °C, exhibiting stable periodic temperature oscillations around a cycle-averaged value of 54.3 °C. Parametric sensitivity evaluations across coolant temperatures of 15 °C, 10 °C, 5 °C, and 0 °C demonstrated a predictable linear downward shift in the mean operating temperature from 64.3 °C to 49.3 °C without altering the constant 4.5 °C thermal oscillation amplitude. These quantitative outcomes validate hybrid air-liquid cooling architectures as an effective, failsafe thermal management solution for next-generation electric vehicle traction inverters, high-voltage power converters, and high-density industrial microprocessors.
